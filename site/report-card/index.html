Bus spacing and bunching

A route can run the promised number of buses and still make riders wait longer when those buses arrive in clumps. These metrics compare consecutive arrivals within the same route, direction, stop, and hour so normal schedule changes do not look like unreliability. Headway CV means coefficient of variation (standard deviation ÷ mean); lower values mean more even spacing.

Bunched arrivals have a realized headway below half the scheduled headway; long gaps exceed 1.5 times the scheduled headway. Only scheduled headways of 40 minutes or less are eligible. Values are percentage points, so 0.1 means 0.1% (about one in 1,000 comparable headways), not 10%. Expected wait assumes riders arrive at random moments; spacing penalty compares observed wait with evenly spaced service at the same realized frequency. Realized gaps below 30 seconds or above 90 minutes are excluded as duplicate and overnight/outlier guards. Method inspired by Salman Khan’s Chicago bus-bunching project, adapted to AC Transit GTFS-Realtime stop arrivals and published GTFS schedules.

How the score is computed

The composite score blends four metrics over the trailing four-week window:

On Time Service Delivered — 40%. Percent of scheduled intermediate passenger-pickup stops reached in the rider window: no more than 1 min early and 7 min late (−60 s ≤ delay ≤ 420 s).

On time — 10%. Percent of arrivals within 0 ≤ delay ≤ 3 min of schedule.

Speed vs. ideal — 15%. Average per-leg bus speed as a fraction of a 13 mph ideal, capped at 100%. Slower routes score lower; avg_speed / 13 × 100.

Bus spacing and bunching — 35%. The sub-score is 100 × (1 − headway CV). Headway CV means coefficient of variation: standard deviation ÷ mean. A CV of 0 is perfectly even spacing; higher values mean increasingly uneven gaps. Method inspired by Salman Khan’s bus-bunching project.

If bunching data is missing, partial, too low-frequency, or statistically insufficient, its component is omitted with a warning and the remaining available weights are renormalized.
